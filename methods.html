Methods & data provenance

Scope

This database catalogs non-coding and regulatory variants (promoter, 5′/3′ UTR, splice-site, intronic, and structural) reported to influence expression or splicing of six ISBT blood group systems: ABO (001), MNS (002), RH (004), KEL (006), FY/Duffy (008), and JK/Kidd (009). RH and KEL are each browsable here by their component genes or antigen groupings, for guide-design convenience: RHD and RHCE (two genes within RH), and the Kpa/Kpb and Jsa/Jsb antigen pairs (within KEL, alongside K/k).

It deliberately excludes common coding-region SNPs that define the classical serological alleles (e.g. the ABO exon 6/7 substitutions) except where a coding variant co-segregates with a regulatory allele background relevant to a listed entry. The intent is to surface the variant classes most useful for guide-design work in genome and base editing, where the accessible target space is often outside the coding sequence.

Data provenance

Entries were compiled by Jun Liu, MD, PhD (Chai Lab, Brigham and Women's Hospital / Harvard Medical School) from three source types:

  • Primary literature: PubMed-indexed case reports, functional studies (reporter assays, minigene splicing assays), and population surveys describing a specific variant's effect on antigen expression. Each record links directly to its source article (see its detail view).
  • ISBT allele tables: the ISBT Blood Group Database, maintained by the International Society of Blood Transfusion Working Party for Red Cell Immunogenetics and Blood Group Terminology, used for system/allele naming and cross-checking allele backgrounds.
  • RhesusBase (rhesusbase.info), for RHD/RHCE allele and haplotype cross-referencing.

Population allele frequencies are pulled from gnomAD v4 and TOPMed where the variant is represented in those callsets; many rare or structural variants are not, and are marked "N/A" rather than assumed absent. Each record with a gnomAD-represented variant links to its specific gnomAD entry (see its detail view).

Field definitions

The columns in the CSV/JSON download and the variant detail drawer, grouped by section. Fields not listed here are self-explanatory (e.g. gene, chromosome) or already defined below (validation tier, audit status, base-editing feasibility).

allele_backgroundThe ISBT allele context the variant occurs on (e.g. B.01, RHCE*Ce), when reported by the source study.
isbt_allele_designationThe full ISBT allele name assigned to this variant itself, when ISBT has issued one. Distinct from allele_background, which describes the surrounding haplotype rather than the variant's own designation.
phenotypeThe reported red-cell antigen phenotype (e.g. Fy(a−b−), Ko). Where a study reports a functional or expression-magnitude effect instead of a discrete antigen phenotype, that effect description is used here and is not a formal phenotype designation.
alternative_notationLegacy or alternate variant nomenclature (e.g. older IVS-based intron numbering) for the same variant. Does not contain curation notes.
regionThe genomic feature the variant falls in (promoter, 5′/3′ UTR, splice site, intron, or structural element).
mutation_typeSNV, small insertion/deletion, or structural-variant class.
molecular_mechanismA prose summary of how the variant is reported to affect expression or splicing. Curatorial audit notes, when present, are appended in brackets, e.g. [AUDIT NOTE: ...].
populationThe population(s) the variant or its frequency estimate was reported in by the source study, when specified.
bystander_editsWhether other editable bases fall within the same base-editing window as the target substitution, when assessed.

Validation tier legend

Each entry's evidence is bucketed into one of five tiers for filtering. The original, more granular wording is preserved in each variant's detail record. Source studies and prior compilations describe evidence strength in inconsistent terms (e.g. "Strong"/"Moderate"/"Inferred" in some entries, named assay types in others); these free-text labels are algorithmically mapped into the five tiers below rather than drawn from a single controlled vocabulary.

Clinical / SerologicalVariant confirmed against patient serology, clinical genotyping, or in vivo phenotype.
Functional / Cell cultureEffect demonstrated in a reporter, minigene, or cell-line expression assay, without direct clinical confirmation.
Database onlyPresent in ISBT/RhesusBase allele tables without an accompanying functional or clinical study cited.
In silico / unvalidatedPredicted regulatory or splice effect (e.g. by a sequence model) with no wet-lab or clinical confirmation.
Not characterizedReported as an allele-defining variant without a described mechanism.

Audit status legend

As part of ongoing curation, every entry is checked against its cited source. The audit label on each record reflects the outcome of that check, not the strength of the underlying biology.

VerifiedReference, coordinates, and stated effect confirmed directly against the primary source.
Likely validConsistent with the primary source on the fields checked; not yet independently re-derived.
CorrectedAn error was found and fixed (a broken URL, a misattributed reference, an incorrect year). The correction is noted on the individual record.
FlaggedA discrepancy was found and not yet resolved (e.g. an unconfirmed PMID, an allele later removed from the ISBT table). Treat with caution until reviewed further.
UnauditedNot yet run through the reference-check pass.

Base-editing feasibility annotations

Where relevant, each variant is annotated for feasibility with two common CRISPR base editors, evaluated against the reference/alternate allele in its genomic (not transcript) strand context:

  • BE4max: a cytosine base editor (C→T on the edited strand).
  • ABE8e: an adenine base editor (A→G on the edited strand).

A rating of Feasible or Medium means the substitution is chemically compatible with that editor in principle; it is not a validated guide design and does not account for PAM availability, bystander edits within the editing window, or off-target risk unless explicitly noted in the record's guide notes field. Deletions, insertions, and structural variants are marked not applicable, since base editors perform single-base substitutions only.

Update cadence

Current data release: 2026-08-13. The dataset is reviewed quarterly for new publications relevant to the six covered systems. Between reviews, individual records may be corrected as discrepancies are found during use; see the Changelog for the dated record of every correction.

Disclaimer

Research use only. This database is a literature-compilation resource intended to support research and assay/guide design. It is not a validated clinical genotyping tool, is not maintained under a clinical quality system, and must not be used alone to inform patient care, transfusion decisions, or genetic counseling. Always confirm findings against the primary literature and, for clinical questions, against an accredited reference laboratory.

About this resource

A research catalog of non-coding and regulatory blood group variants, compiled for the study of variant-guided base editing strategies for antigen modulation. Not a clinical or diagnostic tool.
