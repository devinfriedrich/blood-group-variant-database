About

Why this database exists, who built it, and where it fits in the Chai Lab's work.

What is the Blood Group Variant Database?

The Blood Group Variant Database is a curated compilation of non-coding and regulatory DNA variants reported to affect antigen expression across six ISBT blood group systems (ABO, RH, MNS, KEL, FY, JK). Each entry links a variant to its ISBT allele context, gnomAD v4 and TOPMed population frequency, level of experimental validation, and feasibility for correction with current CRISPR base editors (BE4max, ABE8e). The database is a research compilation, not a clinical or diagnostic tool.

Why this exists

Most blood group nomenclature resources, ISBT's Blood Group Database, RhesusBase, and the now-retired dbRBC (formerly hosted at NCBI), are organized around the coding mutations that define serological alleles. That's the right emphasis for transfusion medicine, but it leaves a gap for a specific downstream use: designing base-editing strategies that modulate antigen expression, where the accessible, single-base-substitution target space is very often a promoter, splice site, UTR, or intronic regulatory element, not the coding sequence itself.

This database exists to close that gap: it pulls together the non-coding and regulatory variants scattered across the primary literature for six blood group systems, cross-references them against ISBT nomenclature and population frequency databases, and annotates each one for base-editing feasibility, so that this variant class is browsable and searchable in one place instead of re-derived from scratch for every project that needs it.

Who's behind it

Jun Liu, MD, PhD compiled and curates the underlying dataset, including the literature search, ISBT/RhesusBase cross-referencing, and the ongoing reference-audit process described on the Methods page.

This resource is hosted under the Chai Lab, Department of Pathology, Brigham and Women's Hospital / Harvard Medical School.

How it's meant to be used

  • Guide design: filter to a gene or antigen grouping and validation tier, then check the base-editing feasibility annotation before designing a guide for a given variant.
  • Literature triage: each entry links back to its source publication; use it as an index into the primary literature rather than a replacement for reading it.
  • Not a clinical tool: this is a research compilation, not a validated genotyping product. See the disclaimer on the Methods page before using it for anything patient-facing.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a "non-coding" or "regulatory" variant here?
Promoter, 5′/3′ UTR, splice-site, intronic, and structural variants reported to affect expression or splicing. Coding-region substitutions that define classical serological alleles are out of scope unless they co-segregate with a listed regulatory variant. Full definition on the Methods → Scope page.

How current is the data?
See the release date on Methods and the dated record of every change on the Changelog.

Can this be used for clinical genotyping?
No. This is a research compilation, not a validated clinical tool. See the Methods page disclaimer.
