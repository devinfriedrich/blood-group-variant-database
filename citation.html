Citation

How to credit this database, and how to credit the individual studies behind each variant.

Citing the database

If this resource informed your research, guide design, or analysis, please cite it as below. This credits the compilation effort; it does not replace citing the original studies for specific variants (see the next section).

Suggested citation

In NLM/Citing Medicine format, the convention used by NCBI resources such as ClinVar and dbSNP:

Liu J. Blood Group Non-Coding Variant Database [Internet]. Boston (MA): Chai Lab, Department of Pathology, Brigham and Women's Hospital, Harvard Medical School; 2026 [cited YYYY Mon DD]. Available from: [database URL].

BibTeX

@misc{liu_bloodgroup_variant_db, author = {Liu, Jun}, title = {Blood Group Non-Coding Variant Database}, howpublished = {Chai Lab, Brigham and Women's Hospital, Harvard Medical School}, year = {2026}, url = {[database URL]}, note = {[cited YYYY Mon DD]} }
URL placeholder. Replace [database URL] above once this site has a permanent home (e.g. a lab-hosted or GitHub Pages URL), and fill in the bracketed citation date with the date you accessed the resource. Until then, cite it as an internal Chai Lab resource.

Citing an individual variant entry

Each variant's underlying finding, whether a case report, functional assay, or population survey, belongs to the researchers who published it. When you use a specific variant record, cite both:

  1. The original publication(s) listed in that variant's Key reference field (with its PubMed link, when available), for the scientific finding itself.
  2. This database, for the compilation, ISBT cross-referencing, gnomAD/TOPMed annotation, and base-editing feasibility assessment.

Every variant's detail panel (open any row in the browser) includes a ready-made citation line combining both, which you can copy directly.

Example: Cai X, et al. [original citation from the record's Key reference field]. Cited in: Blood Group Non-Coding Variant Database, variant ABO-NC-001 (ABO). Compiled by Jun Liu, MD, PhD (Chai Lab, Brigham and Women's Hospital / Harvard Medical School).

Usage & license

This resource is provided for academic and research use. A formal reuse license (e.g. CC BY 4.0) has not yet been assigned by the Chai Lab; until one is designated here, please contact the lab before redistributing the compiled dataset itself. Individual cited studies remain under their original publishers' copyright and licensing terms.

Contact & corrections

Found an error, an outdated ISBT allele designation, or a study we should include? Contact the Chai Lab, Department of Pathology, Brigham and Women's Hospital / Harvard Medical School, attention Jun Liu, MD, PhD.

About this resource

A research catalog of non-coding and regulatory blood group variants, compiled for the study of variant-guided base editing strategies for antigen modulation. Not a clinical or diagnostic tool.
